# Process overview

## What I built

SLOP3251, The Formal Theory of Poker, is a level 3 course. The obvious course under a poker brief is one that makes you better at poker. I built the opposite: a course for people who already trust their instincts and want to know which of them survive being turned into a proof. Each week takes one piece of folklore, a hand is worth playing, a fold was correct, an opponent is bluffing, and either proves it or names it wrong. That choice settled most of what the brief left open. Strategy advice was out. A retraction counts as a result, so weeks revise earlier weeks rather than starting fresh. And it fixed the rule I put in CLAUDE.md before any content existed ([`583b199`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-peacefulmind43/commit/583b199)): any number in a session has to come from a harness that computed it, not from memory.

## How I got here

The obvious order is content first, check it after. I went the other way where the work allowed it: the ICM calculator ([`0cd5918`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-peacefulmind43/commit/0cd5918)) landed as its own commit, before the week 10 content that cites it. Where a harness and its week did ship together, as the Monte Carlo simulator and week 1 did ([`ad47176`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-peacefulmind43/commit/ad47176)), the rule still bound the part that matters: the equity figure on the page came out of the simulator, and `spec/poker-equity.test.ts` recomputes it rather than restating it.

The harder problem is knowing a solver is right when there is nothing to check it against. So the CFR solver ([`c52eaa4`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-peacefulmind43/commit/c52eaa4)) was pointed first at Kuhn poker, which has a published solution. The spec asserts the game value of −1/18 and the whole published strategy family: the lowest card bluffing with some α in [0, 1/3], the highest betting at 3α, the middle calling at 1/3 + α. Only once it reproduced all of that did week 12 turn the same solver on a four-rank version of the game, which has no published answer to check against ([`b458268`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-peacefulmind43/commit/b458268)). A broken solver fails loudly on the game with a known answer, instead of quietly returning a plausible wrong number on the one without.

A smaller call went the same way. The starter scaffold put the one slide deck on week 1, and leaving it there was the easy option. But week 1 is orientation, and a deck earns its place where a reader has to be walked through a calculation a step at a time. So it moved to week 3, the exact-outs and pot-odds week, where each slide advances one line of the computation ([`0900945`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-peacefulmind43/commit/0900945)).

The worst part of the process was a mistake I made. A question bundled three things, image style, the policies page, and this file. I rejected it and typed "keep going", meaning stop showing me that popup, not decide everything yourself. It got read as the second one, and it picked an artwork style alone, deleted two photos, wrote the policy text, and pushed this file without asking again. I reverted both commits ([`a79517d`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-peacefulmind43/commit/a79517d), [`f6bb80a`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-peacefulmind43/commit/f6bb80a)) and redid each decision one at a time, checking the result myself before anything went back in. That redo surfaced two smaller calls: its own SVG artwork over reusing the starter images, and refusing to reuse Minecraft's Steve and Alex designs for staff photos on copyright grounds, deleting the orphaned photos rather than guessing at a replacement ([`a5d096d`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-peacefulmind43/commit/a5d096d)). Since then a call like that gets checked with me first, one at a time, before anything is written or pushed.
