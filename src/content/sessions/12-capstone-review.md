## Before the session

Read the lecture page. Bring the −1/18 published Kuhn poker game value and
the α-parameterized strategy table — the session traces two or three
iterations of regret minimization by hand on one information set, to see
concretely what "shift probability toward whatever was regretted most"
actually does to the numbers.

## In the session

Working in pairs, the group picks one of the twelve Kuhn poker information
sets and manually computes the regret-matched strategy for a few rounds
from an arbitrary starting point, watching it drift toward the published
equilibrium's shape without ever being told the target values in advance.

Second half: the semester's own scorecard, revisited. Each pair defends,
using only what's on the lecture page, whether they'd upgrade or downgrade
this course's own self-assessment of any of the four resolved claims —
arguing with the material rather than just restating it.

## Afterwards

This is the last Problem Session of the semester. Nothing further is
assigned here — the final synthesis this week's lecture ends on is the
last new idea; weekly assignment 12, the midterm, and the final exam are
where it gets tested.
