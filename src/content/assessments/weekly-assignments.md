## The brief

> Compute one quantity — a combo count, an equity, an EV, a Nash frequency, a
> Kelly fraction — and show the working that got you there.

Ten problem sets run across the semester, released after most lectures and
due the following Monday at noon. Each one asks for a single number tied to
that week's material: how many combos are left in a range after the flop,
what a hand's equity is against a stated range, the EV of a call at a given
price, the equilibrium bluffing frequency in a toy game, a fractional-Kelly
stake. The number is checkable — there is a right answer — which is the
point. An answer with no working attached is indistinguishable from a
guess or a number copied from somewhere else, so it isn't accepted as
evidence you can do the calculation.

Your lowest two scores are dropped, which is meant to absorb a bad week
without a policy conversation, not to make the other eight optional.

## What you submit

A short written response per problem set: the final answer clearly stated,
and the steps that reach it (a formula applied, a hand-worked enumeration,
a derivation) in enough detail that a marker can follow it without asking
you to explain it in person.
