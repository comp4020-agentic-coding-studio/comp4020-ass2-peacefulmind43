# Policies and support

## Late work and extensions

Weekly Assignments already drop your lowest two scores, which is the
mechanism for absorbing a single bad week — a short illness, a clash with
another deadline — without a form. Anything larger than that (ongoing
illness, bereavement, a documented emergency) needs an extension request
to the convenor before the deadline, not after it. Undocumented late
submissions to a Weekly Assignment lose 10% of that assignment's mark per
day late, capped at five days, after which it is marked as not submitted.
The midterm and final exam are sat at their scheduled time; a missed exam
needs the same before-the-fact documentation, and is otherwise a zero for
that component.

## Academic integrity

Every assessment in this course asks for a computed number *and* the
working that produced it, so the thing being checked for is the same thing
across all three: is the method shown actually what generated the answer.
Concretely:

- Discussing a method with a classmate is fine; submitting their working,
  or working generated by a solver or language model and presented as your
  own derivation, is not — the whole point of showing your work is that it
  is checkable, and copied work fails that check even when the final
  number happens to be right.
- You may use a calculator for arithmetic. You may not submit a number
  quoted from an equity calculator, a solver, or any other tool in place of
  the derivation this course teaches — quoting a solver's output is exactly
  the move week 12 draws the line under: trustworthy only once the method
  behind it has been checked by hand first, which is what each Weekly
  Assignment is asking you to do.
- Suspected breaches are handled under the university's standard academic
  integrity procedure, not invented here.

## Getting help

Problem Sessions (weekly, see the [Problem Sessions](/sessions/) page) are
where a claim from lecture gets recomputed from scratch — bring a method
you can't get to balance, not just a request for the final number. Outside
of that, office hours and email details are listed on each teacher's
[People](/people/) page; email is for anything that can't wait until the
next session, and course-level questions (extensions, the syllabus, a
policy on this page) go to the convenor rather than a tutor.
