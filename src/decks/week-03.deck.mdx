# Pot Odds, Equity, and the Calling Decision

Week 3 — reopening a Week 1 shortcut, and pricing a real decision with it

---

## Reopening a Week 1 folk rule

Week 1 named "use the rule of 4 and 2 to estimate your equity on a draw" as
a claim to be cashed in later. This is that week.

---

## The rule of 4 and 2

A fast mental-math shortcut for equity on a drawing hand:

- **Two cards to come** (you're on the flop): outs × 4 ≈ equity %
- **One card to come** (you're on the turn): outs × 2 ≈ equity %

No hypergeometric distribution required — just count outs and multiply.

---

## Why a shortcut at all

Exact equity needs a hypergeometric calculation most players can't run at
the table in real time. The rule of 4 and 2 trades precision for speed.

The question this deck answers: **how much precision, exactly?**

## The flagship hand

**Hero:** A♠K♠. **Board (turn):** 5♠ 9♠ 2♥ Q♦.

Two spades in hand, two on board — a nut flush draw, no pair, one card
(the river) to come.

---

## Counting the outs

13 spades total, minus the 4 already visible (2 in hand, 2 on board):

**9 outs.**

---

## Rule of 2 vs exact, one card to come

| Method | Equity |
|---|---|
| Rule of 2 (9 × 2) | 18.0% |
| Exact hypergeometric | 19.6% |

The rule **underestimates** here by about 1.6 percentage points.

---

## Rule of 4 vs exact, two cards to come

Same 9-out draw, but caught on the flop instead of the turn:

| Method | Equity |
|---|---|
| Rule of 4 (9 × 4) | 36.0% |
| Exact hypergeometric | 35.0% |

Now the rule **overestimates**, by about 1.0 percentage point — and the
error grows with the out count:

| Outs | Rule of 4 | Exact | Error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | 36.0% | 35.0% | +1.0 pp |
| 15 | 60.0% | 54.1% | +5.9 pp |

# The rule isn't wrong — it's linear

The real relationship isn't. It tracks well for small draws and drifts
further for big multi-way draws, in a direction and size you can now name.

---

## Outs, by draw type

The flagship hand is one entry in a small catalog. Same counting method,
different shapes:

---

## Gutshot: 4 outs

Hero **Q♣J♦**, board **9♥8♠2♣**. Only a Ten completes 8-9-T-J-Q — one rank,
four cards, none visible: **4 outs**.

---

## Open-ended straight draw: 8 outs

Hero **9♦8♣**, board **7♠6♥2♦**. A 5 or a Ten completes the straight from
either end — two ranks, eight cards: **8 outs**.

---

## Combo draw: 15 outs

Hero **J♠T♠**, board **9♠8♥2♠**. A flush draw (9 spade outs) *and* an
open-ended straight draw (Q or 7, 8 cards) — but the Q♠ and 7♠ are each
already counted as flush outs. Union, not sum:

9 + (8 − 2 overlap) = **15 outs**, not 17.

# Module 3 done

The counting method is stable across draw types — the trap is elsewhere

---

## The miscount that's easy to make

Back to the flagship hand: A♠K♠ on 5♠9♠2♥Q♦. It's tempting to count outs
straight off the board:

13 spades − 2 seen on the board = **11 outs**.

---

## Why that's wrong

Your own hole cards remove supply from the deck too, not just the board.
Hero is holding two of those thirteen spades:

13 − 2 (board) − **2 (hero's own hand)** = **9 outs**, not 11.

---

## What the miscount costs

| Outs used | Equity (1 card to come) |
|---|---|
| 11 (naive) | 23.9% |
| 9 (correct) | 19.6% |

A 4.3-percentage-point overestimate, purely from forgetting to remove your
own cards from the count. This is card removal doing its damage silently,
inside a step that looks like simple arithmetic.

---

## Exact hypergeometric equity: the formula

For `outs` cards among `unseen` remaining, in `draws` cards still to come:

**P(at least one out) = 1 − C(unseen − outs, draws) / C(unseen, draws)**

The complement — the probability of missing *every* out — is easier to
count directly, so the formula counts that and subtracts from 1.

---

## Plugging in the flagship hand

outs = 9, unseen = 46 (52 minus 6 known cards), draws = 1:

P = 1 − C(37, 1) / C(46, 1) = 1 − 37/46 = 9/46 ≈ **19.6%**

---

# A real equity figure

Not a rule of thumb, not a rounded shortcut — an exact answer this deck
will now put a dollar value behind.

---

## The pot-odds threshold

To decide whether a call is profitable, compare your equity against the
**equity a call needs to break even**:

**required equity = call / (pot + call)**

`pot` here already includes whatever the opponent just bet.

---

## The flagship's numbers

Pot is $100 (including villain's bet). Hero must call $50 to see the river:

required equity = 50 / (100 + 50) = 50 / 150 = **33.3%**

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## Verdict: fold

Hero's real equity is 19.6%. The call needs 33.3%. That's not close —
this is the week's first genuine "no," and it's a number, not a feeling.

---

## What implied odds add

If hero expects to win extra money on later streets *when the draw hits*,
that expected extra money counts toward the pot in the threshold formula:

required equity = call / (pot + call + expected future winnings)

---

## The flagship, with $60 implied

required equity = 50 / (100 + 50 + 60) = 50 / 210 ≈ **23.8%**

Still above the flagship's 19.6% equity. Implied odds narrow the gap here —
they don't close it.

---

## How much would it take?

Solving for the implied winnings that make the call break even:

50 / (150 + X) = 9/46 → X ≈ **$106**

That's the concrete size of the implied-odds argument this call would
actually need — not "probably enough," a number.

---

## Reverse implied odds

The mirror problem: sometimes hitting your draw doesn't win the pot — a
bigger flush, a redraw you didn't see. That risk only pushes the real
required equity **higher**, never lower. This deck's numbers so far assume
it away; a careful player doesn't.

## The hand, from the top

Flop: 5♠9♠2♥. Hero calls a small bet holding A♠K♠. Turn: Q♦, no help to
the board's texture. Villain bets $50 into a $100 pot.

---

## An informal range read

Villain's sizing and the line so far look more like top pair or an
overpair than a slowplayed set or two pair — a rough class, not a
distribution. *Week 6 formalizes how to turn a betting line like this one
into an actual range.*

---

## The numbers, side by side

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Hero's exact equity | 19.6% |
| Required equity to call | 33.3% |
| Required equity, with $60 implied | 23.8% |

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## Verdict, stated once

Equity is well short of what a call needs, with or without a reasonable
implied-odds allowance. **Fold** — and now every step of that sentence has
a number behind it.

---

## Pricing the decision in EV

**EV(call) = equity × pot − (1 − equity) × call**

This turns "fold" from a verdict into a dollar figure — what calling is
worth *on average*, not just whether it clears a threshold.

---

## The flagship's EV

equity = 9/46, pot = $100, call = $50:

EV(call) = (9/46 × 100) − (37/46 × 50) = **−$20.65**

---

## What the folk rule would have priced it at

Using the rule-of-2 estimate (18%) instead of the exact figure:

EV(call) = (0.18 × 100) − (0.82 × 50) = **−$23.00**

Same fold either way here — but the folk rule misprices the decision by
about $2.35, and Module 2 already showed that gap only grows on bigger
draws.

---
